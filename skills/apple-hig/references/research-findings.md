## Scope and status

English primary sources were consulted on September 10, 2026. The research retrieved 173 public HIG pages: the home page, six main indexes, eight component indexes, and 158 articles. Working copies are stored temporarily outside the repository. This report preserves conclusions and sources, not copies of the manual.

Textual reading of all 173 pages, including their internal sections, is complete: Getting started (9 articles), Foundations (18), Patterns (25), Inputs (13), Components (64 articles and eight subindexes), and Technologies (29). Content was retrieved from Apple's public DocC documents; navigation and selected destinations were cross-checked in the browser. The coverage tables below identify each page and its usefulness for guiding lookups.

## Findings incorporated into the skill

### The goal determines the route, beyond the control name

A pattern explains the experience; a component describes a piece of that experience. Charting data points to Charts for the elements of a chart; Searching combines in-app search, scope, and Spotlight; Modality presents alternatives according to the task and platform. The skill now instructs the agent to connect these perspectives before recommending an implementation. Sources: [Charting data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charting-data), [Searching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/searching), [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality).

### General pages contain important capabilities

Launching includes launch screens; Offering help includes tips and tooltips; File management includes Quick Look, document launchers, and file extensions; Managing accounts covers passkeys, deletion, and the relationship with subscriptions. Filtering by title may not reveal these topics. The skill adds heading searches and find-in-page. Sources: [Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching#Launch-screens), [Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help#Creating-tips), [File management](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/file-management#Quick-Look-previews), [Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts).

### Platform alone does not define the scope of a recommendation

Digital Crown distinguishes system-reserved functions on Apple Vision Pro from interactions available to apps on Apple Watch. Eyes distinguishes gaze-based hover from keyboard or controller focus. Nearby interactions also depends on hardware and its field of view. The skill accounts for the device, surface, inputs, and usage context. Sources: [Digital Crown](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/digital-crown), [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Nearby interactions](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/nearby-interactions).

### Cross-cutting concerns are distributed across pages

Reading a single page does not address all accessibility concerns: Dynamic Type guidance is in Typography, VoiceOver has its own page, and components contain specific guidance. Permissions have subsections within Privacy. The skill makes these connections visible. Sources: [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography#Supporting-Dynamic-Type), [VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover), [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy#Requesting-permission).

### Link recovery must account for redirects

Designing for iOS still references Spatial interactions. During the browser check, that destination redirected to Nearby interactions; its change log records the rename. An extraction failure does not establish that a topic has disappeared. Sources: [Designing for iOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios), [Nearby interactions](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/nearby-interactions#Change-log).

### Similar names can lead to different experiences

Activity views covers sharing; Live Activities covers tracking an activity. Controls names a surface for system actions. Tab views and Tab bars describe different arrangements, and API names do not always map one-to-one to HIG concepts. The skill adds these distinctions and separates App icons, Icons, SF Symbols, Images, and Image views. Sources: [Activity views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-views), [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls), [Tab views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views), [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons).

Components also contains routes that a title does not reveal: navigation bars in Toolbars; checkboxes and radio buttons in Toggles; scope bars and tokens in Search fields; pull-to-refresh in Progress indicators; menu bar extras in The menu bar; app-specific keyboards and keyboard extensions in Virtual keyboards. These hints were incorporated into the search workflow without turning each subsection into another branch of the map. Sources: [Toolbars → Navigation](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars#Navigation), [Toggles → Checkboxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles#Checkboxes), [Search fields → Scope bars and tokens](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields#Scope-bars-and-tokens), [Progress indicators → Refresh content controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators#Refresh-content-controls), [The menu bar → Menu bar extras](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar#Menu-bar-extras), [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards).

### Choose an integration by role, purpose, and stage

Buying, accepting an in-person payment, selling digital content, verifying identity, and presenting a pass are different needs. Wallet brings together passes, orders, and identity; Photo editing specifically covers extensions in Photos; Maps includes place cards. The table now exposes these functional needs, and the workflow asks the agent to confirm scope before applying recommendations. Sources: [Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay), [Tap to Pay on iPhone](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tap-to-pay-on-iphone), [In-app purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/in-app-purchase), [ID Verifier](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/id-verifier), [Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet), [Photo editing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/photo-editing), [Maps](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/maps).

Design also includes setup, permissions, cancellation, error, and recovery states. For example, Tap to Pay distinguishes reading a card from authorizing payment; In-app purchase includes management and restoration; SharePlay covers participants joining, leaving, and returning. The skill directs the agent to read about these states when they affect the task, without requiring them as a universal checklist. Sources: [Tap to Pay on iPhone](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tap-to-pay-on-iphone), [In-app purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/in-app-purchase), [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay).

For features outside the app, the route depends on whether the person needs an action, confirmation or result, glanceable information, or ongoing progress. Siri, App Shortcuts, and Snippets form a related route; Widgets, Live Activities, and Notifications cover other needs and surfaces. Read the variants for the final surface as well as the platform that initiates the experience. Sources: [Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri), [App Shortcuts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-shortcuts), [Snippets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/snippets), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities).

### Specifications and resources serve different purposes

A decision may require anatomy, specification tables, visual examples, production assets, or implementation documentation. Customization limits vary across integrations; a general ban on custom interfaces should not be inferred. The skill directs the agent to the relevant resource and preserves the distinction between a recommendation, a requirement, and its application to the case. Sources: [Typography → Specifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography#Specifications), [App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons), [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols), [Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple), [CarPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carplay), [Game Center](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-center).

### Retrieving a current page does not guarantee consistency across versions

A specific discrepancy was observed: Mac Catalyst describes the iPad tab bar at the bottom, while the iPadOS section of Tab bars places it near the top. This requires checking versions and context before advising on a port; it does not establish that all Catalyst guidance is invalid. The skill adds comparison with the dedicated page and current documentation, and asks the agent to explain unresolved conflicts. Sources: [Mac Catalyst → Navigation](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/mac-catalyst#Navigation), [Tab bars → iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars#iPadOS).

A stable route does not imply stable content either: change logs record new sections and interactions without changing the page title, such as Look to Scroll in Scroll views. The skill asks the agent to detect scope changes during lookups; change logs help discover them but do not certify a complete update or replace reading the page. Sources: [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views#Change-log).

## Limitations

The research interprets text, tables, headings, links, notices, and textual descriptions of illustrations. It is not an exhaustive visual inspection of all images, videos, or design kits, nor a check of every API or requirement outside the HIG. Retrieving a page today does not establish that every paragraph is current for the same system version. Numerical values and specifications remain in Apple's sources.

| Request | Verified short route and rationale | Result |
|---|---|---|

**Assessment:** 5/5 requests have a useful route without invented pages or automatic assumptions about platform applicability. The Foundations row remains dense, but the reading hints and need-oriented terms were sufficient to locate Writing and Privacy quickly.

**Limitations:** This was a qualitative evaluation by an agent that had already researched part of the corpus; it does not measure automatic triggering in new sessions or demonstrate general performance. Images, videos, and live APIs were not inspected. The routes describe the necessary reading, not final design validation.
