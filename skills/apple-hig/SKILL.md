# Apple Human Interface Guidelines

Use Apple's live HIG to answer design questions and guide implementation or review. The table below provides topic discovery; the linked pages supply the design guidance. Apply platform-specific advice to the user's target platform and OS version, including web contexts when the relevant Apple integration supports them.

## Find and apply guidance

1. Identify the user's design decision, target platform, and relevant OS version from the task. Account for the actual device, window or system surface, input methods, and usage context when they affect the decision. A platform name alone may not distinguish a handheld app, a resizable window, an immersive space, or a glanceable system experience. Ask only when missing context would materially change the recommendation; otherwise state any necessary assumption.
2. Use **What the user needs** to identify candidate topics. For a broad design task, begin with the platform's Getting started page and the relevant Foundations. For a focused question, open the specific topic directly. Read the live parent index when exploring available guidance or checking a route; it may expose topics added since this table was verified.
3. Read the introduction and scope notes, scan the page's internal headings, then read the sections relevant to the decision. Guidance may live under anatomy, variants, behavior, lifecycle, accessibility, specifications, or a platform-specific subsection instead of Best practices. Read applicable notes, tables, and examples together; inspect illustrations when appearance, anatomy, or correct/incorrect usage matters. Follow related HIG topics when they could change the decision.
4. Check platform considerations alongside restrictions in the body and the actual device capabilities. “No additional considerations” is not an API availability guarantee. When implementation details matter, follow developer-documentation links and confirm version-sensitive APIs there. When a page refers to required branding assets, entitlements, or App Review requirements relevant to the task, follow the source that owns those requirements. Use linked design resources for production assets and videos for additional rationale or demonstrations, checking their dates and platform context.
5. Answer the actual task: recommend a design, explain a tradeoff, review the supplied interface, or provide a reading route as requested. Cite the specific live pages supporting material recommendations. Distinguish Apple's explicit guidance from your application of it, and preserve the strength of Apple's wording when describing a recommendation or requirement.

Finish when the design decision is supported by the relevant guidance and applicable platform constraints. Scale the reading to the question instead of traversing the whole directory. For a reading request, explain why each selected page matters and the useful reading order.

## Choose a reading route

Use the six sections as complementary perspectives on a decision:

- **Getting started:** Platform conventions and the physical context of use, including ergonomics, viewing distance, and session length.
- **Foundations:** Visual and interaction qualities that cut across features, including accessibility, privacy, language, layout, and materials.
- **Patterns:** A person's activity or flow, including entry, interruptions, feedback, and recovery.
- **Components:** The anatomy, variants, behavior, and customization of a particular interface element or system surface.
- **Inputs:** How people target and operate the experience, including alternate input methods and system-reserved interactions.
- **Technologies:** The user-facing workflows, boundaries, and handoffs of an Apple integration.

Connect the relevant perspectives rather than treating the categories as exclusive. Charting data helps choose how to communicate information; Charts covers the chart itself. Searching covers the search experience and Spotlight; Search fields covers the control, placement, and scoping. Managing notifications addresses delivery and interruption; Notifications addresses presentation and actions. For a proposed modal interface, read Modality before selecting Sheets, Alerts, Popovers, or Action sheets. For custom interaction, consult the relevant Inputs topic and accessibility guidance as well as the component.

Follow cross-cutting concerns to their specific homes: Dynamic Type is in Typography, VoiceOver has its own page under Technologies, and permission-request flows are in Privacy. Read these when the task involves text scaling, assistive navigation, or access to personal data; their location in another section should not hide their relevance.

Resolve ambiguous names before choosing a page. App icons covers product identity; Icons covers interface glyphs and document icons; SF Symbols covers the symbol library and its rendering; Images covers assets, whereas Image views covers their container. Activity views concerns sharing; Live Activities concerns ongoing tasks. Controls concerns system controls, not every control in an app. Tab views and Tab bars describe different arrangements; choose using their platform context. Photo editing addresses extensions in Photos rather than every image editor.

For integrations, identify the user's role and the stage of the flow. Buying, accepting an in-person payment, selling a digital subscription, verifying identity, and presenting a pass can lead to different Technologies pages even when all involve Wallet or payment terminology. Read the relevant setup, permission, cancellation, error, recovery, and system-handoff sections when those states are part of the task. For actions outside the app, consider Siri, App Shortcuts, Snippets, Controls, Widgets, or Live Activities according to whether the person needs an action, a result, glanceable content, or ongoing progress.

## Navigate and search

Start at the [HIG home](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines) or a linked topic. The documentation navigator groups topics into six main sections; expand the relevant branch or use its **Filter** field to locate a topic by name. Clear the filter when returning to the wider directory. This topic filter is distinct from the global Apple Developer **Search** control; inspect the current UI labels rather than relying on saved selectors or positions.

The navigator's topic list does not enumerate all guidance inside pages. Use the page's contents links, headings, or find-in-page to locate a concept before concluding that it is missing. For example, launch screens are inside Launching, tips and tooltips inside Offering help, Quick Look and document launchers inside File management, and account deletion inside Managing accounts. Use a section's actual anchor when linking to it; if an anchor changes, reopen the parent page and locate the current heading.

Other useful internal entry points include Liquid Glass in Materials, navigation in Toolbars, checkboxes and radio buttons in Toggles, scope bars and tokens in Search fields, refresh content controls in Progress indicators, menu bar extras in The menu bar, custom input views versus custom keyboards in Virtual keyboards, place cards in Maps, and order tracking and identity verification in Wallet. These are search hints, not a second page hierarchy. Preserve Apple's topic names for discovery while explaining the relevant guidance in the user's language.

If the user's wording is conceptual, translate it into candidate topic names using the table. For example, “show delivery progress on the Lock Screen” suggests Live Activities; “compare monthly spending” suggests Charts and Charting data. If the navigator doesn't locate the subject, use global search or a web query such as `site:developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines <topic or design problem>`. Open the matching official page and verify its relevance; search snippets alone do not establish current guidance. Discover URLs from the live site or search results when a topic is absent from the table.

Apple's documentation may return a JavaScript shell to text retrieval tools. Use an available browser to read the rendered page and its visual examples when needed. If live access remains unavailable, explain that current guidance could not be verified; provide useful candidate links or clearly qualified general advice without claiming to have read the pages or confirmed a change.

## Recognize changes and recover

Treat the live HIG as authoritative over this navigation baseline. During relevant lookups, check that the destination still covers the intended topic and that any parent path used still matches the live index. Read page change logs when available to understand a move, split, or scope change. For requests about recent changes or a skill refresh, also consult Apple's [What's new in design](https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/) and follow its relevant HIG links.

Live retrieval does not guarantee that related pages describe the same OS generation. If guidance appears inconsistent, compare the precise scenario, platform, version, and change-log entry with the dedicated topic and current developer documentation. Prefer evidence explicitly applicable to the target context; explain any remaining conflict instead of combining incompatible guidance or choosing solely by page date. Treat change logs and What's new as discovery aids, not proof that every paragraph or linked resource has been updated.

A failed fetch or empty JavaScript shell is an access problem, not proof that Apple removed a topic. For a missing or unexpected destination, try the live parent index or HIG search to find its current home. Continue the user's task using verified replacement guidance when possible.

Notify the user when an observed addition, rename, move, removal, or changed scope affects this table, its routing instructions, or a recommendation made in the task. Include the stored expectation, the observed page or route with a source link, the effect on the task, and the smallest proposed skill update. Distinguish confirmed differences from suspected ones. A valid URL alone does not prove that its guidance is unchanged, and this skill stores no historical page contents for a full semantic comparison.

Report actionable differences together at the end of the task. Routine wording or artwork changes that leave the skill's navigation and advice unaffected do not require a skill update. Checks happen during use; this skill does not monitor Apple between invocations. Propose maintenance when needed, and edit the skill when that maintenance is within the user's requested scope. When refreshing it, verify the affected routes and state the scope and date of that verification; reserve a new whole-table baseline date for a complete index check.

For maintenance or a review of why these routes exist, consult the [research findings and coverage](references/research-findings.md). This dated evidence supports skill updates; use live Apple pages for current design advice.
